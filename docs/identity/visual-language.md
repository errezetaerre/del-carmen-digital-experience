# Vision

Del Carmen Digital Experience is not designed to imitate a conventional museum, online gallery or e-commerce platform.

It is designed as a contemplative digital environment where art, light, silence and technology coexist naturally.

The experience should feel closer to entering an artistic world than navigating a traditional website.

Technology must remain present through precision, responsiveness and interaction, but visually subordinate to the artwork.

Every visual decision must reinforce that experience.

---

# Core Principles

## Contemplation Before Interaction

The artwork is always the protagonist.

The interface exists to reveal, contextualize and connect.

It must never compete for attention.

Interaction should emerge naturally after contemplation.

---

## Simplicity Over Decoration

Every visual element must have a purpose.

Nothing exists merely to make the interface appear more designed.

Decoration without narrative, structural or atmospheric purpose should be removed.

Simplicity does not mean emptiness.

It means intentional presence.

---

## Silence as Design

Silence is a visual material.

Negative space creates rhythm, hierarchy and anticipation.

Darkness can function as negative space.

Whitespace does not necessarily mean a white surface.

It means visual breathing room.

Space should feel intentional rather than excessive.

---

## Timelessness Over Trends

The visual language should remain relevant beyond current interface trends.

Avoid fashionable effects that quickly identify a specific technological era.

The experience should feel contemporary without becoming trend-dependent.

---

## Art Before Interface

The visitor should remember the artwork before remembering the interface.

UI elements should progressively disappear from conscious attention.

The interface succeeds when navigation feels natural but the emotional memory belongs to the art.

# Composition

Composition should feel editorial rather than application-driven.

Layouts may be asymmetric when the artwork benefits from asymmetry.

Not every scene needs to be centered.

Not every scene needs identical proportions.

The visual system should create coherence without forcing every section into the same composition.

Large artwork may intentionally dominate one side of the viewport.

Text may occupy a quieter region.

Negative space may carry as much compositional importance as visible content.

---

# Spatial Rhythm

The experience should breathe.

However, breathing room must reinforce narrative continuity.

Large empty areas should not exist merely to create a perception of luxury.

Spacing should create:

Pause

Transition

Hierarchy

Anticipation

Contemplation

Sections that belong closely together may use tighter transitions.

Important contemplative scenes may use more generous spacing.

The Home should feel like one continuous journey rather than a stack of disconnected full-screen presentations.

---

# Darkness

Darkness is one of the primary visual materials of the current Del Carmen experience.

It creates silence around the artwork.

Dark surfaces should not feel flat or digitally black.

Subtle differences between near-black surfaces may be used to distinguish scenes without visually separating the experience.

Darkness should provide depth, not heaviness.

Pure visual emptiness should be avoided when atmosphere can create a more natural transition.

---

# Light

Light is directional.

Light reveals.

Light creates hierarchy.

Light establishes atmosphere.

It should behave more like illumination in a painting or exhibition environment than a decorative digital effect.

Light may be used to:

Reveal artwork

Guide attention

Create depth

Separate visual planes

Support transitions

Reinforce emotional tone

Light should never become the subject of the interface.

---

# Atmosphere

Atmosphere may emerge through:

Subtle gradients

Vignettes

Controlled haze

Soft glow

Directional illumination

Depth

Slow motion

Atmospheric effects must remain nearly imperceptible when the visitor is not actively looking for them.

The objective is not to make the visitor notice an effect.

The objective is to make the environment feel alive.

---

# Color

The artwork should normally remain the most colorful element on screen.

The interface uses restrained neutral environments to support it.

The current Home is predominantly dark. Subtle near-black surface changes may distinguish adjacent scenes while preserving continuity.

Primary Gold identifies selected visual focal points.

Muted Bronze primarily communicates interaction and secondary emphasis.

Gold and bronze should behave as accents, not decorative themes.

They should never spread across large areas of the interface.

Light surfaces such as Smoke Ivory may appear in future editorial contexts when intentionally required, but they do not define the current Home environment.

Atmospheric highlights

Gold must remain scarce enough to retain meaning.

Typography should feel spacious but not artificially expanded.

Artwork is not content decoration.

It is the primary visual material of the platform.

Artwork should therefore be treated differently from ordinary website imagery.

Preserve:

Color

Detail

Visual integrity

Rounded artwork corners

# Artwork Visual Representations

An Artwork is a single canonical artistic entity even when different visual assets are required to present it across the experience.

Visual representations must not create duplicate Artwork records. They are context-specific representations of the same work.

Canonical representation roles are:

Primary

The faithful default representation of the artwork. It is the preferred source for artwork detail, Featured Artwork and Collection contexts unless a more specific representation is intentionally required.

Hero Portrait

An optional composition prepared for portrait Hero environments when the artwork requires different framing, negative space, atmosphere or integration with brand copy.

Hero Landscape

An optional composition prepared for landscape Hero environments. When no dedicated Hero Landscape representation exists, Primary is the canonical fallback.

Thumbnail

An optional optimized representation for compact cards, previews or discovery surfaces. A thumbnail must preserve the identity and visual integrity of the artwork.

The existence of a Hero-specific representation does not imply that the artwork itself is exclusive to the Hero.

Hero treatments may introduce surrounding darkness, gradients, negative space or atmospheric integration when necessary for composition, but they must not materially alter the artwork itself.

Every meaningful artwork image representation must provide appropriate alternative text. Alternative text belongs to the representation and should describe the visual content accurately without unnecessary promotional language.

The interface should prefer the most context-appropriate representation while preserving Primary as the canonical visual source and fallback.

# Artwork, Series and Curatorial Presentation

The visual language distinguishes between an individual Artwork and an Artwork Series.

An Artwork represents one artistic work.

An Artwork Series represents a curated body of related works connected through concept, subject, narrative, visual language or artistic intention.

A Series may use one artwork as its visual cover without reducing the Series to that individual work.

When a Series appears in a discovery or Collection context, its presentation should communicate that the visitor is entering a broader body of work rather than opening only the cover artwork.

Home placement is curatorial rather than intrinsic to the identity of an artwork. Hero, Featured Artwork and Featured Collection may therefore use different works, and repetition should occur only when it reinforces the intended narrative.

The Home should avoid unnecessary repetition of the same artwork across consecutive scenes.

# Motion

Motion exists to guide attention and create continuity.

It should never become the focus.

Motion should feel:

Slow

Natural

Atmospheric

Controlled

Purposeful

Motion may support:

Scene arrival

Artwork revelation

Navigation transitions

Subtle environmental movement

Interactive feedback

Atmospheric light

Future immersive experiences

---

# Motion Hierarchy

The artwork remains visually dominant even when motion exists around it.

Motion should generally occur in the environment or interface rather than making the artwork perform unnecessarily.

Avoid:

Constant movement

Decorative looping

Aggressive parallax

Fast scaling

Exaggerated easing

Attention-seeking transitions

Motion should stop being consciously noticeable shortly after it begins.

---

# Interaction

Interactions should feel effortless.

The visitor should understand what is interactive without requiring conventional heavy UI treatment.

Interaction may be communicated through:

Typography

Opacity

Underline behavior

Subtle color change

Gold or bronze transition

Small-scale transformation

Controlled glow

Cursor behavior when appropriate

Interactions should never produce visual noise.

---

# Buttons and Links

Del Carmen primarily uses typographic interaction rather than conventional solid buttons.

Editorial CTAs may use restrained underline treatments.

Navigation links remain cleaner and should generally avoid decorative lines.

Legal links remain simple.

Social interaction is icon-based.

Solid application-style buttons should appear only when functionality genuinely requires them.

---

# Icons

Icons should feel integrated into the visual environment.

They should remain:

Minimal

Recognizable

Quiet

Monochromatic by default

Social icons may use the intrinsic filled geometry of their original SVG design.

They should not use native social-platform colors.

Interaction may introduce restrained gold emphasis or glow.

Icons must never become decorative focal points.

---

# Interface Visibility

The interface should become quieter as the visitor enters deeper into the artwork.

Navigation, labels and controls should occupy only the visual weight necessary for orientation.

The platform should avoid persistent interface chrome when it does not serve the visitor.

The ideal state is not the absence of interface.

It is the absence of interface awareness.

# Relationship With the Design System

This document defines visual intent.

DOC-DT defines the canonical implementation tokens.

DOC-HS defines the Home experience and behavior.

DOC-HW defines spatial architecture.

Implementation should consume these systems rather than independently recreating visual decisions.

When implementation reveals a recurring visual behavior not yet represented by the system, that behavior should be evaluated for promotion into the Design System.

Not every local compositional decision requires a global rule.

# Design Principle

Everything unnecessary disappears.

The artwork remains.

Technology supports the experience.

Light creates atmosphere.

Typography gives voice.

Space creates silence.

Motion creates continuity.

Interaction creates connection.

---

# Golden Rule

Every visual decision must reinforce contemplation.

If an element competes with the artwork, remove it.

If an effect becomes more memorable than the painting, reduce it.

If technology becomes the protagonist, reconsider it.

The visitor should remember the art before remembering the interface.
