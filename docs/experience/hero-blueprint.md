Document Type: Experience Blueprint
Authority Level: Highest
Status: 🟢 Approved
Owner: Del Carmen Digital Experience
 
Purpose
Define the complete experiential journey through the Del Carmen Home.
The Blueprint connects the Home sections into one continuous narrative.
It defines:
•	narrative progression, 
•	emotional rhythm, 
•	visual hierarchy, 
•	transitions, 
•	visitor attention, 
•	interaction philosophy, 
•	pacing, 
•	and the relationship between sections. 
It does not replace the Home Specification.
It does not replace the Home Art Direction.
It connects them.
 
Core Experience
The Home should not feel like a conventional webpage.
It should feel like entering a digital exhibition.
The visitor should feel that they are moving through a sequence of carefully curated spaces.
The experience should breathe.
The interface should remain secondary.
The artwork should remain the protagonist.

Section 01
Hero
Narrative
Arrival
Purpose
Introduce the visitor to Del Carmen.
Create curiosity without immediately explaining everything.
The visitor should slow down.
Primary protagonist
Featured Artwork
Secondary elements
•	DEL CARMEN 
•	Painting the Eternal Essence Within 
•	Artistic statement 
•	Navigation 
•	Floating artwork information 
Visual behavior
The artwork dominates the scene.
The brand remains on the left.
The composition follows the approved grid:
6% | 30% | 54% | 10%
The artwork should feel large, physical and museum-like.
Interaction
Minimal.
The visitor is primarily invited to contemplate.

Transition

The artwork, light and atmosphere gradually lead into the next experience.

Pacing
The sections should not all have identical heights.
The visitor should intuitively feel when a scene requires more time.
Approximate starting rhythm:
Hero                 100–115vh

Featured Artwork      90–120vh

About Artist          90–110vh

Collections           120–160vh

Journal               90–110vh

Collectors            80–100vh

Contact               70–90vh
These values are art-direction targets, not rigid implementation requirements.
The composition takes priority over arbitrary viewport measurements.
 
Transition Philosophy
There should never be an obvious visual "cut" between sections.
Transitions should use combinations of:
•	light, 
•	opacity, 
•	scale, 
•	position, 
•	atmosphere, 
•	typography, 
•	artwork movement, 
•	negative space. 
The visitor should feel movement through a continuous environment.
 
Scroll Philosophy
The scroll is the visitor's movement through the exhibition.
It should never feel like simply moving down a long webpage.
Scroll-driven animation should therefore be:
•	smooth, 
•	deliberate, 
•	restrained, 
•	spatial, 
•	meaningful. 
Avoid excessive parallax.
Avoid constant movement.
Avoid animation for decoration alone.

Navigation Philosophy
Navigation should remain available without dominating the composition.
The current concept:
•	Logo/brand area acts as the navigation trigger. 
•	Navigation appears when the visitor approaches the designated area. 
•	Menu items receive a subtle background when active/hovered. 
•	Active items may use an underline. 
•	Navigation disappears when it is no longer needed. 
The navigation should feel discovered rather than permanently imposed on the visitor.

Experience Rule
The Home must always answer three questions without explicitly asking them:
Where am I?
Inside the Del Carmen artistic world.
What should I look at?
The current protagonist of the scene.
Why should I continue?
Because the next experience has not yet been revealed.

# Experience Blueprint Evolution Update — 2026-10-05

This section extends the supplied v1.0 Hero Blueprint without deleting, compressing or silently rewriting its original Home journey.

The original DOC-HB v1.0 body remains preserved above as the Highest-authority approved experiential blueprint.

# Scope Clarification

Despite its historical title, `Hero Blueprint` defines substantially more than the Hero.

Its stated purpose is to connect the complete Home into one continuous experiential narrative.

Version 1.1 therefore preserves the document name and ID for continuity while clarifying its practical role:

`DOC-HB = Home Experience Blueprint`

The Hero remains the opening chapter of that journey.

This clarification does not rename the file or create a duplicate document.

---

```text
ARRIVAL
→ CONTEMPLATION
→ THE ARTIST
→ DISCOVERY
→ REFLECTION
→ BELONGING
→ FAREWELL
```

and the original scene mapping:

```text
Hero
→ Featured Artwork
→ About the Artist
→ Collections
→ Journal
→ Circle of Collectors
→ Contact
```

That original sequence remains preserved above.

Subsequent approved Home documentation evolved the current scene architecture to:

```text
Hero

→ Artist Statement

→ Selected Works

```

The later scene architecture supersedes the v1.0 scene mapping for the current Home implementation.

# Hero Composition Evolution

The v1.0 Hero records an approved historical grid:

`6% | 30% | 54% | 10%`

That grid remains preserved as part of the original blueprint.

Subsequent responsive Home implementation has evolved through breakpoint-specific composition and approved visual tuning.

Therefore the historical grid should not be interpreted as a universal rigid geometry that overrides the later approved responsive composition.

The deeper Hero rules remain canonical:

• artwork dominates;
• brand and information remain subordinate;
• atmosphere and negative space create visual silence;
• the Hero introduces rather than explains;
• the visitor should want to continue;
• the interface should recede behind the artwork.

---

# Featured Artwork

The original Contemplation role remains valid.

Featured Artwork should provide a sustained encounter with an individual Artwork rather than behaving as a promotional card.

The progression:

```text
Artwork
→ Detail
→ Title
→ Story
→ Meaning
```

remains an experiential possibility rather than a requirement that all information appear in that exact sequence.

The current Home Specification governs actual content and behavior.

---

# Artist Statement

The current `Artist Statement` scene evolves the original `About the Artist` blueprint role.

Its experiential purpose remains human connection.

The Home should reveal enough of the artist's voice and philosophy to create intimacy and meaning without duplicating the full Artist/About experience.

The current approved Home documents govern its actual content.

---

# Discovery — Featured Collection and Selected Works

The original `Collections` scene has evolved into two distinct Home experiences:

```text
Featured Collection
→ threshold into one ArtworkSeries

Selected Works
→ curated individual Artwork records
```

Together they continue to fulfill the original Discovery role.

Featured Collection should feel like entering a broader artistic world rather than selecting an e-commerce category.

Selected Works should feel curated rather than exhaustive.

The current canonical domain relationship is:

`Featured Collection → ArtworkSeries`

Visitor-facing `Collection` terminology remains editorial and does not create a duplicate persistence model.

---

# Reflection — Journal Preview

The original Journal principle remains valid:

`This is not simply a blog. It is an artistic journal.`

The current Home uses `Journal Preview` rather than the full Journal experience.

Its role is to introduce thought, process and reflection while preserving the visitor's Home journey.

Reading remains the primary behavior when the visitor enters the full Journal experience.

The absence of a dedicated canonical Journal document in the current documentation package remains a reconciliation gap, not a cancellation of Journal.

---

# Belonging — Invitation and Future Collector Experience

The original `Circle of Collectors` scene introduced belonging, relationship, exclusivity, discovery and participation.

The current Home architecture expresses the closing relationship through the broader `Invitation` scene rather than a dedicated Collector scene.

This supersedes the original Home placement.

It does not cancel the future Collector ecosystem.

Collector accounts, collector relationships, marketplace/commerce pathways and other future roadmap capabilities remain valid future platform directions.

The Invitation may later connect to those capabilities when they exist and when the Home narrative justifies the connection.

---

# Farewell — Footer and Contact

The original Contact scene carried the Farewell role.

The current Home architecture separates the closing `Invitation` from the `Footer`.

The Footer now completes the exhibition-like departure.

The dedicated Contact experience remains part of the platform and is not cancelled by its removal as a standalone Home scene.

The original desired feeling remains valid:

`I would like to return.`

---

# Emotional Rhythm

The v1.0 principle that Home must alternate intensity and silence remains canonical.

Current scenes should not be normalized into equal visual intensity or identical viewport height.

Stillness is an active part of the rhythm.

Movement gains meaning because quieter scenes surround it.

The current motion vocabulary may include:

• Stillness
• Atmospheric Reveal
• Materialization
• Spatial Drift
• Luminous Accent
• Portal Transition

These terms extend the original transition philosophy without replacing it.

---

# Pacing and Viewport Targets

The v1.0 approximate `vh` ranges remain preserved as historical art-direction targets for the original scene structure.

They are not rigid implementation requirements.

Because the current Home structure has evolved and responsive compositions differ by breakpoint, exact scene height should continue to follow composition, content and emotional pacing.

The original rule remains authoritative:

`The composition takes priority over arbitrary viewport measurements.`

---

# Scroll and Direct Interaction

The original Scroll Philosophy remains valid:

scroll is the visitor's movement through the exhibition.

Scroll-driven motion should remain deliberate, restrained, spatial and meaningful.

Subsequent Del Carmen interaction language also includes direct manipulation in experiences where horizontal exploration has a real spatial purpose.

This does not mean Home must adopt Artwork Detail kinetic physics or KineticCarousel behavior.

Home should reuse kinetic mechanics only when a Home scene genuinely requires that interaction.

Natural vertical scrolling must remain available and should not be obstructed by decorative gesture capture.

---

# Interaction Progression

The original principle that interaction changes across the journey remains valid.

However, the exact v1.0 mapping:

```text
Hero → Minimal
Artwork → Subtle
Artist → Minimal
Collections → Moderate
Journal → Functional
Collectors → Intentional
Contact → Simple
```

belongs to the original seven-scene architecture.

For the current Home, interaction intensity should be evaluated per approved scene while preserving the deeper rule:

`Not every scene should demand interaction.`

The experience must never feel like a game or motion demonstration.

---

# Marketing Philosophy and Future Commerce

The original commercial principle remains fully valid:

The Home may encourage commercial action without feeling like a sales funnel.

The visitor should encounter art, meaning and trust before transactional pathways dominate attention.

Future Marketplace, Collector, edition, print, order, payment and related commerce capabilities may extend this journey.

Their absence from the current Home does not cancel them.

When introduced, they should inherit the original progression from relationship toward invitation rather than reversing the Home into a storefront-first experience.

---

# Navigation Philosophy Evolution

The original proximity-triggered navigation concept remains preserved as an approved historical concept.

Current navigation behavior should follow the latest approved implementation and accessibility requirements.

A historical interaction concept should not override a later canonical navigation implementation solely because it appears in the v1.0 blueprint.

The deeper navigation principles remain:

• available without dominating;
• discoverable without becoming obscure;
• visually subordinate to artwork;
• understandable across input methods;
• accessible.

---

# Responsive Experience

The original rule remains canonical:

`The composition may change. The story does not.`

Desktop, tablet and mobile may use different compositions and interaction mechanics.

Mobile must not be treated as compressed desktop.

The current approved responsive Home implementation governs exact breakpoint composition.

The emotional sequence, protagonist hierarchy and visitor orientation should remain coherent across devices.

---

# Future Experience Evolution

This Blueprint remains a narrative bridge between Home Specification and Home Art Direction.

Future Home evolution may add, remove, split, combine or reinterpret scenes through explicit approved decisions.

The blueprint should then evolve while preserving traceability.

Future platform modules such as Collector experiences, Marketplace, Virtual Museum, exhibitions, Academy, Community, immersive environments and other roadmap capabilities are not required to appear as Home scenes merely because they exist in the ecosystem.

Home should remain curated.

It is an entrance into the Del Carmen universe, not a dashboard of every platform capability.
