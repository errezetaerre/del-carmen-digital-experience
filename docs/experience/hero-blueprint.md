Purpose
Define the complete experiential journey through the Del Carmen Home.
The Blueprint connects the Home sections into one continuous narrative.
It defines:
•	narrative progression, 
•	emotional rhythm, 
•	visual hierarchy, 
•	transitions, 
•	visitor attention, 
•	interaction philosophy, 
•	pacing, 
•	and the relationship between sections. 
It does not replace the Home Specification.
It does not replace the Home Art Direction.
It connects them.
 
Core Experience
The Home should not feel like a conventional webpage.
It should feel like entering a digital exhibition.
The visitor should feel that they are moving through a sequence of carefully curated spaces.
The experience should breathe.
The interface should remain secondary.
The artwork should remain the protagonist.

Section 01
Hero
Narrative
Arrival
Purpose
Introduce the visitor to Del Carmen.
Create curiosity without immediately explaining everything.
The visitor should slow down.
Primary protagonist
Featured Artwork
Secondary elements
•	DEL CARMEN 
•	Painting the Eternal Essence Within 
•	Artistic statement 
•	Navigation 
•	Floating artwork information 
Visual behavior
The artwork dominates the scene.
The brand remains on the left.
The composition follows the approved grid:
6% | 30% | 54% | 10%
The artwork should feel large, physical and museum-like.
Interaction
Minimal.
The visitor is primarily invited to contemplate.
Navigation may reveal itself through proximity to the brand/logo area.
Transition
The Hero does not abruptly end.
The artwork, light and atmosphere gradually lead into the next experience.
 
Section 02
Featured Artwork
Narrative
Contemplation
Purpose
Give the visitor time to connect with the artwork.
The artwork receives more space than information.
Primary protagonist
The Artwork
Experience
The visitor moves from seeing the artwork to discovering its meaning.
Possible progression:
Artwork
   ↓
Detail
   ↓
Title
   ↓
Story
   ↓
Meaning
Information should be revealed progressively.
Never overwhelm the visitor with a technical specification immediately.
Interaction
Subtle movement.
Artwork details may respond to scroll or cursor position.
No unnecessary effects.
Transition
The visual focus gradually shifts from the artwork to the person who created it.
 
Section 03
About the Artist
Narrative
The Artist
Purpose
Create human connection.
The visitor has seen the work.
Now they meet the person behind it.
Primary protagonist
Del Carmen
Content
•	Portrait 
•	Artistic statement 
•	Short biography 
•	Artistic philosophy 
•	Link to full artist page 
Visual direction
Editorial.
Quiet.
Personal.
Never corporate.
Interaction
Minimal.
Typography and imagery should carry the experience.
Transition
Understanding the artist creates curiosity about the broader body of work.
 
Section 04
Collections
Narrative
Discovery
Purpose
Allow the visitor to explore the artistic universe.
Collections should feel like different rooms rather than categories in an e-commerce store.
Primary protagonist
The Collections
Experience
Each collection should feel like entering another environment.
For example:
Collection

↓

Atmosphere

↓

Selected Works

↓

Explore
Visual direction
Large imagery.
Strong negative space.
Distinct atmospheres.
Minimal labels.
Interaction
This is one of the areas where interaction can increase.
Hover, movement, transitions and spatial navigation may become more expressive.
However, the interaction must remain intuitive.
Transition
After discovering the work, the visitor is invited deeper into the artist's thoughts.
 
Section 05
Journal
Narrative
Reflection
Purpose
Allow visitors to enter the intellectual and creative world of Del Carmen.
This is not simply a blog.
It is an artistic journal.
Primary protagonist
Ideas
Content
•	Reflections 
•	Creative process 
•	Stories 
•	Exhibitions 
•	Artistic inspiration 
•	Behind-the-scenes material 
Visual direction
Editorial.
Quiet.
Readable.
More typographic than visual.
Interaction
Simple navigation between stories.
Reading should remain the primary activity.
Transition
Reflection creates familiarity and trust.
That prepares the visitor for a deeper relationship with the artist.
 
Section 06
Circle of Collectors
Narrative
Belonging
Purpose
Introduce the collector relationship.
The visitor should feel invited rather than pressured.
Primary protagonist
The Collector
Experience
The message should communicate:
•	access, 
•	relationship, 
•	exclusivity, 
•	discovery, 
•	participation. 
The language should never feel like aggressive sales copy.
Avoid making registration feel like a transaction.
Interaction
This section may introduce more explicit actions.
For example:
•	Join 
•	Discover available works 
•	Receive private updates 
•	Access collector information 
Transition
The visitor has reached the most intimate part of the journey.
The Home can now begin to close.
 
Section 07
Contact
Narrative
Farewell
Purpose
End the experience naturally.
The visitor should not feel that they have reached a generic website footer.
They should feel that the exhibition has ended.
Primary protagonist
The Invitation
Experience
Simple.
Personal.
Quiet.
Possible elements:
•	Contact 
•	Newsletter 
•	Social presence 
•	Collector invitation 
Visual direction
Return toward darkness.
Reduced visual complexity.
Large negative space.
Final feeling
The experience should leave the visitor with:
"I would like to return."

Beauty needs contrast.
 
Pacing
The sections should not all have identical heights.
The visitor should intuitively feel when a scene requires more time.
Approximate starting rhythm:
Hero                 100–115vh

Featured Artwork      90–120vh

About Artist          90–110vh

Collections           120–160vh

Journal               90–110vh

Collectors            80–100vh

Contact               70–90vh
These values are art-direction targets, not rigid implementation requirements.
The composition takes priority over arbitrary viewport measurements.
 
Transition Philosophy
There should never be an obvious visual "cut" between sections.
Transitions should use combinations of:
•	light, 
•	opacity, 
•	scale, 
•	position, 
•	atmosphere, 
•	typography, 
•	artwork movement, 
•	negative space. 
The visitor should feel movement through a continuous environment.
 
Scroll Philosophy
The scroll is the visitor's movement through the exhibition.
It should never feel like simply moving down a long webpage.
Scroll-driven animation should therefore be:
•	smooth, 
•	deliberate, 
•	restrained, 
•	spatial, 
•	meaningful. 
Avoid excessive parallax.
Avoid constant movement.
Avoid animation for decoration alone.

Marketing Philosophy
The Home should encourage commercial action without feeling like a sales funnel.
The progression is:
Attention
   ↓
Contemplation
   ↓
Connection
   ↓
Discovery
   ↓
Trust
   ↓
Desire
   ↓
Invitation
The visitor should first fall in love with the experience.
Then discover the work.
Then understand the artist.
Then become interested in collecting.
 
Navigation Philosophy
Navigation should remain available without dominating the composition.
The current concept:
•	Logo/brand area acts as the navigation trigger. 
•	Navigation appears when the visitor approaches the designated area. 
•	Menu items receive a subtle background when active/hovered. 
•	Active items may use an underline. 
•	Navigation disappears when it is no longer needed. 
The navigation should feel discovered rather than permanently imposed on the visitor.
 
Responsive Experience
The narrative remains consistent across:
•	Desktop 
•	Tablet 
•	Mobile 
The composition may change.
The story does not.
On smaller screens:
Artwork
   ↓
Brand
   ↓
Information
   ↓
Narrative
rather than forcing the desktop composition into a narrow viewport.
 
Experience Rule
The Home must always answer three questions without explicitly asking them:
Where am I?
Inside the Del Carmen artistic world.
What should I look at?
The current protagonist of the scene.
Why should I continue?
Because the next experience has not yet been revealed.
