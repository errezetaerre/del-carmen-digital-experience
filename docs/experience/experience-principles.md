# Purpose

Define how visitors should experience Del Carmen Digital Experience.

This document governs every interaction, animation, transition, visual decision and navigation pattern throughout the platform.

Technology serves the experience.

The experience never serves technology.

---

# Experience Vision

Del Carmen Digital Experience is not designed as a traditional website.

It is designed as a digital exhibition where every screen feels intentional, calm and immersive.

Visitors should never feel overwhelmed.

Visitors should never feel lost.

Visitors should always feel invited to continue.

---

# Core Experience Principles

## Contemplation Before Interaction

Silence creates attention.

The interface never competes with the artwork.

---

## Every Interaction Has A Purpose

Interactions exist only when they improve understanding, emotion or navigation.

Nothing moves simply because it can.

---

## Progressive Discovery

Information is revealed progressively.

The visitor is rewarded with discovery instead of being overloaded.

Curiosity becomes part of the experience.

---

## Invisible Guidance

The interface quietly guides visitors.

It never interrupts them.

It never demands attention.

It simply accompanies the journey.

---

## Breathing Space

Empty space is part of the design.

Silence is not absence.

Silence allows the artwork to breathe.

---

## Emotional Design

Every decision should support emotion before functionality.

Functionality must remain effortless.

Emotion creates memory.

---

## Timeless Experience

Avoid trends.

Avoid visual noise.

Avoid effects that will look dated in a few years.

Design should feel elegant regardless of time.

---

# Narrative Sequence

Every visitor should naturally progress through the following emotional journey.

Silence

↓

Curiosity

↓

Contemplation

↓

Emotional Connection

↓

Discovery

↓

Trust

↓

Belonging

↓

Action

The objective is not immediate conversion.

The objective is emotional connection.

Action becomes the natural consequence.

---

# Navigation Philosophy

Navigation should never dominate the interface.

It exists to help.

Not to compete.

Whenever appropriate, navigation may reveal itself only when the visitor demonstrates intent.

Navigation should always remain intuitive.

Mystery must never reduce usability.

---

# Motion Philosophy

Motion should communicate.

Motion should guide.

Motion should breathe.

Motion should never distract.

Animations should feel cinematic rather than technological.

---

# Information Hierarchy

The artwork is always the primary subject.

Brand comes second.

Information comes third.

Controls come last.

If an interface element competes visually with the artwork, it should be redesigned.

---

# User Experience Goals

Visitors should feel...

"I want to stay."

"I want to explore."

"I want to understand."

"I want to know the artist."

"I want to own one of these works."

---

# Experience Evaluation

Every future feature should answer the following questions.

Does it improve contemplation?

Does it reduce friction?

Does it help visitors discover something naturally?

Does it respect silence?

Does it strengthen emotional connection?

Does it preserve elegance?

If the answer is no, the feature should be reconsidered.

---

# Final Principle

Technology should disappear.

The visitor should remember the artwork.

Not the interface.

# Experience Evolution Update — 2026-10-05

This section extends the supplied v1.0 Experience Principles without deleting, compressing or replacing its original experience philosophy.

The original principles above remain the Highest-authority approved foundation.

---

# Kinetic Experience Language

Motion in Del Carmen is not decorative animation.

When interaction moves an artwork, a gallery or an entire scene, the movement should communicate spatial continuity, intention and physical presence.

The visitor should feel that the interface responds to them rather than performs for them.

Kinetic interaction therefore follows the existing principle:

Technology serves the experience.

The experience never serves technology.

---

# The Artwork Has Weight

For direct artwork exploration, the governing experiential principle is:

`The artwork must feel as though it has weight.`

This means movement should feel connected to the visitor's gesture and to the perceived physical presence of the artwork.

The experience should avoid:

• weightless sliding
• arbitrary acceleration
• decorative overshoot without meaning
• a visible stop followed by an unrelated second movement
• motion that makes the interface more memorable than the artwork

Release may preserve momentum when that continuity feels physically coherent.

Resistance at a boundary should communicate that the visitor has reached an edge rather than behaving like an error.

This principle describes experiential intent.

Exact thresholds, velocities, distances, easing curves and implementation constants remain local technical decisions unless repeated use later justifies promotion into the global design system.

---

# Direct Manipulation Before Automation

When a visitor is actively dragging, swiping or using a horizontal trackpad gesture, the scene should respond directly to that interaction.

The visitor's gesture owns the moment.

Automatic motion should not fight against active manipulation.

After release, the system may complete or restore the movement in a way that preserves the trajectory and emotional continuity of the gesture.

This relationship should feel natural enough that the visitor does not need to understand the mechanics behind it.

---

# Spatial Continuity

Where an experience represents movement between neighboring artworks, the transition may preserve a sense that the neighboring work already exists beyond the current viewport.

The next experience should feel discovered through space rather than summoned by an abrupt interface replacement.

This principle currently informs Artwork Detail kinetic navigation and shared horizontal artwork exploration.

It does not require every Del Carmen experience to become horizontal or kinetic.

Stillness remains equally important.

---

# Stillness and Movement

Del Carmen uses both stillness and motion as narrative materials.

Stillness supports:

• contemplation
• reading
• observation
• emotional pause
• the sense of a museum or quiet exhibition environment

Movement supports:

• discovery
• transition
• spatial relationship
• visitor intent
• passage between connected works or experiences

Motion should enter only when movement adds meaning.

A scene that benefits from rest should remain still.

---

# Museum and Portal Rhythm

The experience may alternate between two complementary states.

Museum / Rest

The visitor is given space to observe, read and remain with the artwork.

Portal / Movement

The visitor crosses into another artwork, series, story or experience through purposeful transition.

The contrast between rest and movement creates rhythm.

Continuous animation everywhere would weaken that rhythm.

---

# Progressive Interaction Learning

Interaction guidance should remain discreet and temporary whenever possible.

A visitor may initially receive a restrained invitation such as:

`SWIPE TO EXPLORE`

or:

`SWIPE / DRAG TO EXPLORE`

Once the visitor demonstrates the interaction, the experience should trust that learning rather than repeatedly demanding attention.

Persistent controls or guidance may remain when they serve orientation, accessibility or navigation, but should stay visually subordinate.

---

# Contextual Continuity

The visitor's path into an artwork can carry experiential meaning.

Entering an artwork from a Series may preserve that Series as the immediate exploration context.

Entering independently may preserve a broader archive context.

The artwork itself remains the same artwork.

The interface adapts to the journey without changing the identity of the work.

This supports progressive discovery while avoiding duplicate experiences or unnecessary interruption.

---

# Edge Behavior as Guidance

When movement reaches the first or last valid item, the experience should communicate the boundary through restrained resistance and return rather than through confusing reinterpretation.

An invalid gesture should not unexpectedly become movement in the opposite valid direction.

Boundaries should feel understandable without requiring explanatory UI.

This is an application of Invisible Guidance.

---

# Input Coherence

Trackpad, pointer drag, touch swipe and keyboard navigation may express the same underlying spatial relationship through different input methods.

Their mechanics do not need to be identical.

Their meaning should be coherent.

Accessibility and alternative input must remain compatible with the same narrative structure.

---

# Reduced Motion

Reduced-motion behavior remains part of respectful experience design.

When motion is reduced, the visitor must retain:

• orientation
• navigation
• access to artwork
• contextual continuity
• functional clarity

The emotional intent should survive even when kinetic expression is minimized.

---

# Future Experience Evolution

These principles describe the current experiential language without limiting future roadmap phases.

Virtual Museum, immersive experiences, exhibitions, mobile experiences and other future modules may introduce new spatial or interaction systems.

Those systems should inherit the foundational principles above while adapting their mechanics to the requirements of the future experience.

A future experience should not imitate the current kinetic implementation merely for visual consistency.

It should preserve the deeper principles:

• contemplation before interaction
• purposeful movement
• invisible guidance
• emotional continuity
• art before interface
• technology in service of experience
